The starting point

A customer database decays on its own. Duplicates pile up. Addresses go out of date. Segments created three years ago no longer match anyone.

Yet campaigns keep being sent to it. And people are surprised that results go down.

What was put in place

  1. Look at the data

    First, look at the data before talking about campaigns. Records are audited, duplicates removed, sources reconciled. It is the thankless part. Without it, everything else is wrong.

  2. Define groups with the business

    Then, define groups with the business, not with the tool. A group that exists because the tool can compute it is useless. A group that matches a situation the business can describe gets results.

  3. Build the journeys and measure them

    Finally, build the journeys and measure them. Who gets what, when, and why. Then compare with the starting point, not with a hunch.

You stay in controlGroups are defined with the business, and every journey is compared with the starting point, not with a hunch.

The result

+5% customer retention.

What it taught us

Data quality decides the result, long before how sophisticated the tool is.
