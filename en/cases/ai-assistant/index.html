The starting point

Useful information is rarely where you work. A request comes in by message. The detail is in an email. The date is in the calendar. The follow-up ends up in a spreadsheet.

None of this is hard. But someone spends an hour a day moving that information around.

Three things came back every week:

  • Everything had to be explained again. No tool remembered the previous day’s conversation.
  • Tasks slipped. A follow-up to send, something to monitor, and nobody to think of it at the right time.
  • Failures went unnoticed. When a login expired, the tool answered "nothing found" instead of saying it was broken.

What was put in place

  1. One single conversation

    First, one single conversation. The assistant is reachable from the everyday messaging app. It keeps the thread. Three days later you pick up where you left off.

  2. It actually acts

    Then, it actually acts. It reads email, checks the calendar, opens documents, pushes code, drives a browser. Every sensitive action stops at an approval step. It suggests, you confirm, it acts.

  3. It watches itself

    Finally, it watches itself. It checks on its own that its access works. If it goes down, a watchdog restarts it. It logs its decisions in a journal and rereads it so it does not make the same mistake twice.

What it taught us

The hard part was not the quality of the answers. That comes quickly. The real work is elsewhere: restarting after a failure, saying "I am broken" rather than "I found nothing", and never acting alone on what matters.
